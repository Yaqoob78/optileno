# Optileno — 30-day plan, day by day

**Starting point:** zero users, no network, a finished product, a landing page.
**The one goal:** find out, with real people, whether freelancers use "Can I say yes?" every week and will pay for it.

| By day | Target |
|---|---|
| 7 | 10 people have tried it; you've spoken to 5 of them |
| 14 | 40 tried; 10 came back a second time |
| 21 | 80 tried; 20 use it weekly; first "I'd pay for this" said unprompted |
| 30 | 150 tried; 25+ weekly users; **first 3–5 paying founding members** |

These are targets, not promises. What matters is learning which messages, places and people work.

---

## Every single day (the non-negotiables, ~90 minutes)

1. **15 personal messages** to freelancers (templates in `GO-TO-MARKET.md`). Every one starts with a real line about *their* work.
2. **Reply to everyone within a few hours.** Every reply is a potential user and a source of feedback.
3. **Log everything** in your tracker: who, where, replied, tried, came back, their exact words.
4. **One useful public comment** where freelancers talk (X, Reddit, LinkedIn). Help first; mention Optileno only if it genuinely answers the question.
5. **Before bed:** write one sentence, "Today I learned ___."

---

## Week 1 — Launch quietly, get the first 10 users

**Day 1 — Go live and test like a stranger**
- Make sure the latest version is deployed on optileno.com.
- Open it on your phone and laptop in a private window. Do the whole flow: onboarding → add 3 real projects → "Can I say yes?" → log time.
- Write down anything confusing, even small things.
- Paste the link into WhatsApp and X to check the new preview image shows.

**Day 2 — Set up your tools**
- Create the tracker (Google Sheet). Columns are in `GO-TO-MARKET.md`.
- Update your X and LinkedIn bios: *"Building Optileno — know if you can say yes to your next client. Free."*
- Ask me to add anonymous page-view analytics, so you can see visits and whether people reach the app.

**Day 3 — Record the demo**
- Make a 30–45 second screen recording: type a real-looking request → "Not by Friday" → "Promise next Friday" → copy the reply.
- No music, no intro. Just the moment.
- This clip goes in DMs, posts and replies for the whole month.

**Day 4 — Build your first target list**
- Find **50 freelancers**: web/UI designers, video editors, motion designers.
- Sources: X search ("freelance designer" + "booked", "deadline", "client"), Dribbble/Contra "available for work", LinkedIn "Freelance Web Designer".
- Save the name, link and one detail about their work for each.

**Day 5 — First 15 messages**
- Use template 1 (cold DM).
- **Don't** ask them to sign up. Ask one question: *"When a client asks 'can you start next week?', how do you decide?"* Then share the link.

**Day 6 — Talk to anyone who replied**
- Ask for a 10-minute call, or chat in DMs.
- Watch someone use it if you can: screen share, don't help, stay quiet, note where they hesitate.

**Day 7 — Review week 1**
- Count: messages sent, replies, people who tried.
- Pick **the top 3 confusions** you saw. Send them to me to fix.
- Ask yourself: which message got the most replies? Which niche replied most?

## Week 2 — Fix what confused people, open a second channel

**Day 8 — Fix the top 3 confusions** (I build, you test).

**Day 9 — Follow up**
- Message everyone who tried it (template 4): "Did the answer match your gut? What annoyed you?"
- Copy their exact words into the tracker.

**Day 10 — First public post: Indie Hackers**
- Post: *"I kept saying yes to clients and missing deadlines, so I built a tool that says 'not by Friday' for me."*
- Include the demo clip. Ask for honest feedback.

**Day 11 — Double down on your best niche**
- Spend today's 15 messages on the niche that replied most in week 1.

**Day 12 — Reddit (carefully)**
- Read the rules of r/freelance, r/graphic_design, r/web_design and r/editors.
- Where "show your project" or feedback threads are allowed, share the story (template 3). Elsewhere, only answer questions.

**Day 13 — Content day: one X thread**
- "5 signs you're about to miss a client deadline (and what I do now)."
- Real, useful advice. Optileno appears once at the end.

**Day 14 — Review week 2**
- Numbers: tried, came back 2+ times, what they said.
- **Decision:** is one niche clearly better? From here, focus 70% of your messages on it.

---

## Week 3 — Find people who'd pay

**Day 15 — The pricing question**
- Ask your 5 most active users: *"If this cost $5/month, would you keep using it? What would make it worth $10?"*
- Write down the answers word for word.

**Day 16 — Show HN**
- Post "Show HN: Optileno — know if you can say yes to a new client (free, local-first)".
- Hacker News likes the tech angle: local-first, a real scheduling algorithm, a 3D landing page driven by the engine.

**Day 17 — Ask for proof**
- Ask 3 happy users: *"Can I quote what you told me on the website?"*
- Only real quotes, with their permission, from real people. Never invent one.

**Day 18 — Founding-member offer**
- Ask me to add a "Founding member — $5/month for life" button (Lemon Squeezy checkout).
- It tests willingness to pay with real money.

**Day 19 — Offer it to your weekly users**
- Message each active user personally: "You're one of the first 25 — want to lock the founding price?"

**Day 20 — Partnerships**
- Find 5 freelancer newsletters, YouTubers or communities (small ones, 1k–20k followers).
- Offer: free lifetime Pro for their audience's first 20 sign-ups in exchange for a mention.

**Day 21 — Review week 3**
- Did anyone pay, or say "I'd pay" without being asked?
- If **nobody** would pay: ask 10 users why. Is it too simple, or is the pain not strong enough?
- Bring me the answers; we decide together whether to deepen the product or adjust the niche.

---

## Week 4 — Double down on what works

**Day 22 — Build the #1 requested feature**
- Only if at least 3 different users asked for it. Likely candidates:
  - sync across devices
  - weekly summary email
  - share a schedule link with a client

**Day 23 — Prepare the Product Hunt launch**
- Gallery images: hero, the "Not by Friday" moment, the 3D week.
- Tagline: *"Know if you can say yes to your next client."*
- Tell everyone you've talked to that it's coming.

**Day 24 — Launch on Product Hunt**
- Be online all day. Reply to every comment.
- Personally message users who liked it to try it (don't ask for upvotes; that's against the rules).

**Day 25 — Follow up with every new sign-up from the launch**

**Day 26 — Case study**
- Write up one real user's story: "How Priya stopped double-booking launch weeks" (a real person, with permission).
- Post it on X, LinkedIn and Indie Hackers.

**Day 27 — Second niche test**
- Spend today's messages on the *next* best niche (e.g. if designers worked, try video editors).

**Day 28 — Clean up**
- Answer every open message.
- Update the landing page with real quotes and the real founding-member price.

**Day 29 — The numbers**
- Visitors → tried → came back → paid.
- Which channel brought the users who stayed?

**Day 30 — Decide the next 30 days**

| If… | Then next month… |
|---|---|
| People come back weekly **and** some pay | Scale the channel that worked. Build Pro (sync). Aim for 50 paying. |
| People come back but won't pay | Find what's worth paying for (ask 15 users). Test a higher-value feature. |
| People try it once and never return | The pain isn't sharp enough in this niche. Try the next niche before changing the product. |

---

## Rules for the month

- **Never** fake reviews, user counts or testimonials. One real quote beats a hundred fake ones.
- **Talk to users more than you code.** Every product change should trace back to something a user said.
- **Don't add features because a competitor has them.** Add them because three real users asked.
- **Protect the 90 minutes.** Outreach every day beats a big push once a week.
